Fortune: Microsoft Will Release a Handheld Xbox in 2027, and the Project Helix Console May Cost Up to $1,800

According to Fortune, Xbox will repeat a two-step strategy: first its own handheld console, a year later the full Helix. Meanwhile, debates over the software platform are under way inside Microsoft.

FRAGLINE OFFICIAL29 October 2026
2 min read
Contents

Microsoft’s plans for the next generation of Xbox have become a little clearer. On October 20, Fortune published a lengthy piece on Xbox’s new head Asha Sharma and the division’s strategy. According to the outlet’s anonymous sources, the company is preparing a next-generation handheld console for the 2027 holiday season, while the traditional Project Helix console will arrive roughly a year later, in 2028. Microsoft has not officially confirmed these dates.

Its own handheld instead of the ROG Xbox Ally

According to the report, this is a Microsoft device of its own, not a new model in the vein of ASUS’s ROG Xbox Ally. That is an important difference: the current Allys run Windows 11 and do not run Xbox console games, whereas the future handheld, as Fortune hints, should support the entire Xbox ecosystem. Sharma herself previously told employees that some next-generation Xbox devices would be developed in-house and some together with partners.

The most painful detail concerns Helix’s price. According to internal estimates cited by Fortune, the console could cost buyers between $1,200 and $1,800 unless Microsoft decides to subsidize its cost. The ongoing component shortage also weighs on the forecast.

The Windows vs. Xbox dispute

Fortune also describes an internal conflict. Windows leadership reportedly wanted the handheld to run a stripped-down version of Windows Home, while Sharma’s team insisted on its own software platform for better gaming performance. One of Xbox’s top executives, according to the outlet, even threatened to shut down the handheld project if the Windows option had to be used. Now, it is claimed, the direction of the OS is mostly determined by Xbox.

“We can’t build Helix alone.”

Asha Sharma, Xbox CEO, in an interview with Fortune, Thurrott.com

Sharma stressed that the teams collaborate closely. Xbox CTO Scott Van Vliet even publicly disputed the description of the conflict: on X he wrote that Helix OS is built on top of Windows and that the version of events in the article distorts both the technical work and the relationships between teams.

Against a backdrop of layoffs

The report came at a difficult moment for Xbox. According to Fortune, Sharma plans three more rounds of layoffs, in late October, in November and in January. As a reminder, in July she already announced the elimination of 3,200 jobs at Xbox, of which 1,600 people were affected immediately. So ambitious hardware plans coexist with strict austerity, and Helix’s final price will depend largely on whether Microsoft is ready to invest in subsidies.
