STAR WARS: Galactic Racer: Critics Give It 88 and Praise a Racer That Isn’t Afraid to Be Brutal

A roundup of critics' opinions on Galactic Racer: 88 on Metacritic for the PS5 version, a spread across platforms and complaints about repetitive locations.

FRAGLINE OFFICIAL31 October 20263
3 min read
Game
STAR WARS: Galactic Racer
Platforms
PC, PS5, Xbox Series X|S
Developer
Fuse Games
Release
2026-10-06
Contents

This is not our review but a roundup of critics’ opinions based on Metacritic data. FRAGLINE OFFICIAL’s thesis is this: Galactic Racer won not through the license but by daring to take on a risky design — a roguelike campaign in a racing game — and critics appreciated it. But the price of the risk is also visible: repetitive locations and on-foot segments drag the scores down.

What the numbers say

The game came out on October 6, 2026 on PS5, Xbox Series X|S and PC. The developer is Fuse Games and the publisher is Secret Mode. According to Metacritic, the PS5 version has 88 points from 76 reviews, Xbox Series X has 86 from 15 reviews, and PC has 85 from 27. The user score on PS5 is 8.1 from 249 ratings. Metacritic calls this “generally favorable reviews.”

The spread between platforms is small, but it exists: console is ahead, PC behind. This is a case where the number of reviews per platform differs greatly, and these three figures shouldn’t be compared head-on: the sample on PC and Xbox is noticeably smaller.

What gets praised

Critics agree on the main thing: speed, collisions and the sense of risk. Eurogamer, per Metacritic, gave a maximum 100 and noted the beauty of the worlds and tracks that constantly change the nature of the challenge. Edge gave 90 and called the game “deliriously happy.” Game Rant also gave 90 and advises playing it again and again. The reviews separately single out the roguelike campaign structure, deep customization of vehicles and parts, and the sound and visuals.

“The best Star Wars racing game ever made.”

Metro GameCentral, 90 points, Metacritic

The phrase is loud, but the caveat in it is important: it is specifically about racing in this universe, not about the genre as a whole.

What gets criticized

  • Repetitive environments. Julien Chièze (85), for example, writes about this: the speed and customization are good, but the locations and on-foot episodes wore him out.
  • On-foot sections. They break the pace of the race; other reviews complain about them too.
  • A limited set of modes. Respec (70) would like more classic modes and a fuller Podracing.
  • Player complaints. According to user reviews on Metacritic, irritants include “rubber-band” difficulty at low levels, random negative effects carrying over from race to race, and a weak story.

FRAGLINE OFFICIAL’s take

The best thing Fuse Games did was to abandon the safe “championship on a loop” scheme. The roguelike structure gives each race a stake, and the stake makes the race interesting even on a familiar track. When reviewers write “you want to replay it again and again,” they are essentially describing exactly this: a loop where losing is not useless.

The weak spot is visible in the same reviews: a roguelike lives on variety, and this one lacks locations. The complaint about repetitive environments is more serious here than in an ordinary racing game, because the player has to return to the same planets dozens of times. Add on-foot inserts that break the rhythm, and it becomes clear why reviewers’ scores run from 70 to 100 rather than clustering around 88.

For comparison: on our site we recently covered the roundup for Phantom Blade Zero, which also has 88 on Metacritic. But there the high score rests on flawless combat, while here it rests on a bold structure that worked differently for each critic. We wouldn’t be surprised if the user rating dipped over time as players reach the end of the campaign. But for now the 88 looks deserved, and the PC version is worth waiting on for patches: the roundup shows that scores are lower there.

Sources: Metacritic (game and critic pages). We have not played Galactic Racer and rely only on published reviews.
