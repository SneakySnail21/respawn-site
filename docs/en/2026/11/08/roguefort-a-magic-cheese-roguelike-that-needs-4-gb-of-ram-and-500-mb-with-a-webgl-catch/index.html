Roguefort: a Magic-Cheese Roguelike That Needs 4 GB of RAM and 500 MB — With a WebGL Catch

An Early Access RPG at $9.99 promises an open world, four-player co-op and a fortress mode on modest requirements. What “a GPU with WebGL 2.0” actually means for a low-end PC.

FRAGLINE OFFICIAL8 November 20266
3 min read
Contents

A 500 MB game with an open world, a fortress mode and four-player co-op sounds like a fantasy for an old laptop. Roguefort is exactly that pitch: it landed on Steam in Early Access on October 13 for $9.99. The thesis is simple: the requirements really are modest, but the key line in them is “a GPU with WebGL 2.0”, not “Core i5 processor”, and that line will decide whether the game runs on your hardware.

What the game is

Roguefort was made by a solo developer, Timothy Graham. According to the Steam description, it is an open-world RPG where magic comes from cheesemaking: you can explore a living continent, join guilds, crawl dungeons, build a homestead or go sailing. There is solo play and co-op for up to four, a survival mode and a Fortress mode in which you manage people, farms and workshops. The author defines the genre roots himself like this:

“It’s in the tradition of Caves of Qud and Dwarf Fortress, and the cheese is played completely straight.”

Timothy Graham, developer, as quoted by GamingOnLinux

Requirements: what is stated and what isn’t

The Steam minimum requirements: Windows 10, an Intel Core i5 or AMD equivalent, 4 GB of RAM, a GPU with WebGL 2.0 support and 500 MB of disk space. We found no recommended specs, and the processor generation is not given, so “i5” could mean a fresh chip or a decade-old model. The sources are silent on FPS, and we do not invent numbers.

Note the WebGL mention. According to GamingOnLinux, the game is built on Chromium web technology. That explains both the tiny size and the odd graphics requirement: the graphics are rendered the way they are in a browser. A side effect is described too: the article’s author reports that the Steam Overlay doesn’t work for him on desktop Linux (Fedora KDE), and a reader in the comments mentions a similar problem with another Chromium-based game. For Windows users that is not critical, but it shows the cost of this approach.

Comparison with a reference point

The author points to Caves of Qud and Dwarf Fortress. These games are known as deep systems with simple presentation, and Roguefort is trying to reach the same feeling. On hardware we can honestly compare only with another find of ours this week: Pillars of Eternity asks on Steam for 14 GB of disk space and a Radeon HD 4850, far more space, but it is an old, well-understood classic. Roguefort is 28 times smaller in disk footprint, but requires support for a modern graphics API, even if at browser level.

FRAGLINE OFFICIAL’s take

What works is that a solo developer chose a technology that makes installation almost weightless. For a laptop with a couple of free gigabytes that is a decisive argument, and the Fortress mode with its farms and settlers promises dozens of hours at minimal size. Just over nine dollars for Early Access is not an aggressive price, either.

What is questionable is something else: Early Access remains Early Access, and our source has no scores for the game. The WebGL 2.0 requirement may turn into a trap for the oldest laptops with integrated graphics: formally they “don’t need” a discrete card, but the driver has to support the right version. We did not run the game and can’t verify this ourselves, so before buying we suggest opening any WebGL 2.0 demo in a browser to see whether it works. It is a quick and free test.

Our forecast: if the author keeps up the pace of updates and doesn’t inflate the requirements, Roguefort could become the very “light” indie that gets mentioned in lists for weak PCs. If the game starts growing in size and ambition, the 500 MB promise will stop being its main trump card. For now it is an intriguing experiment with an honestly stated price and an honestly stated minimum.
