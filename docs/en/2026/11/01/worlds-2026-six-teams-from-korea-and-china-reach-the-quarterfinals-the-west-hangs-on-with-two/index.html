Worlds 2026: six teams from Korea and China reach the quarterfinals, the West hangs on with two

The Worlds 2026 Swiss stage is over: a quarterfinal bracket with no lower bracket, three slots for LCK and LPL and one each for LEC and LCS.

FRAGLINE OFFICIAL8 hours ago
3 min read
Contents

The Worlds 2026 Swiss stage has ended, and the picture is this: of the eight playoff participants, six represent Korea and China, while the West hangs on with two teams — Karmine Corp from the LEC and LYON from the LCS. Our thesis: a format without a lower bracket makes these two spots not just “representation” but the only chance to show that the gap between regions hasn’t turned into a verdict.

Who advanced

Dplus KIA and Gen.G finished the Swiss stage 3–0. T1, Invictus Gaming and Top Esports advanced at 3–1. Bilibili Gaming, Karmine Corp and LYON picked up the last spots at 3–2. So LCK and LPL got three slots each, LEC and LCS one each.

Eliminated were Team Liquid, who lost to LOS, G2 Esports (0:2 to Hanwha Life), Hanwha Life itself (0:2 to Bilibili Gaming), Movistar KOI (a loss to Karmine Corp) and LOS (a loss to LYON). There’s an interesting detail here: in the last round Bilibili Gaming knocked out Hanwha Life, meaning a Korean team was eliminated by a Chinese one, not a Western one.

The quarterfinal bracket

All four series are best-of-five, one per day, at 14:00 Pacific time. On November 3, Top Esports play Invictus Gaming (LPL versus LPL), on November 4 Gen.G meet LYON, on November 5 Dplus KIA play Bilibili Gaming, and on November 6 T1 face Karmine Corp.

The only intra-regional match is the first, which means at least one Chinese team is guaranteed to reach the semifinals. The other pairings are clashes of different regions: Korea versus the US, Korea versus China, Korea versus Europe.

“Unlike double elimination, there is no lower bracket here.”

Strafe, Worlds 2026 Playoffs

What this means for the West

The line about the lower bracket matters: Karmine Corp and LYON have no right to make a mistake. Losing a best-of-five series means the end of the tournament, and the opponents for both teams are top-three squads from the Swiss stage: Gen.G went 3–0, T1 went 3–1. For the viewer it’s beautiful drama; for the organizations, an expensive bet: did they get through because they’re strong, or because the Swiss stage gave them a second chance?

Note that both Western teams exited at 3–2, meaning both paths to the playoffs were on the edge. This can be read two ways. On the one hand, they proved their resilience in elimination matches. On the other, Gen.G and T1 came into the quarterfinals with a margin in their record that Karmine Corp and LYON don’t have. The comparison is direct: Dplus KIA and Gen.G went through without losses, while the nearest Western competitor finished with two losses.

FRAGLINE OFFICIAL’s take

What works: the tournament stays interesting even after G2 and Team Liquid have gone. Two Western slots are still alive, and there’s no reason for boredom in the bracket — the very first day promises a clash of Chinese teams, and the last day features Korea versus Europe. The best-of-five-a-day format reduces noise: no fatigue from parallel matches, all attention on one series.

What’s questionable: the bracket structure doesn’t help the weakest. One series — and a team is out with no right to a rematch, while the regional disproportion (three versus one) sets expectations in advance. If Karmine Corp or LYON lose, talk of a “chasm between regions” will flare up again, although in reality they may simply have drawn the main favorite of the Swiss stage.

How it could end: we aren’t predicting winners, but note that the probability of a semifinal without Western teams is high given the bracket structure, while a surprise from LYON or Karmine Corp would change the whole discussion about the strength of the regions. For the West, this is the only way to turn “we made it” into “we can.”
