Resident Evil 2: Deluxe Edition on Switch 2 Scores 93 on Metacritic: A Port Critics Wouldn’t Fault Even on the Small Stuff

A roundup of critic opinion: the Switch 2 Deluxe Edition of the Resident Evil 2 remake holds a 93 on Metacritic from 16 reviews, none of them negative.

FRAGLINE OFFICIAL7 November 20264
3 min read
Game
Resident Evil 2
Platforms
Nintendo Switch 2
Developer
Capcom
Release
October 16, 2026
Contents

First, a disclaimer: this is a roundup of critic opinion based on Metacritic, not our own review. The thesis is simple: the 93 for Resident Evil 2: Deluxe Edition on Nintendo Switch 2 is, above all, a verdict on the game itself and on Capcom’s careful work, not a sensation. The port has earned “universal acclaim”, but the sample is still small, and the number should be read with that in mind.

What the aggregator shows

The game came out on October 16, 2026, with Capcom as both developer and publisher. The Switch 2 version has a Metascore of 93 from 16 reviews, all of them positive. Users give it 8.6 from 71 ratings, 89% of them positive. The gap between critics and players is small, and that combination is not all that common.

The ceiling is 100 from Nintendo Insider. Most other reviewers sit in the 90–95 range: NintendoWorldReport and Gaming Age gave 95, while Nintendo Life, Worth Playing, Gfinity and Gaming Boulevard gave 90. The digest contains almost no small complaints, which is telling: when a port is received this consistently, critics simply have nothing to argue about.

«A modern classic finally gets the treatment it deserves»

Nintendo Life, Metacritic

What is inside the Deluxe edition

Gaming Boulevard singles out the Deluxe extras: an option to play with the original soundtrack and additional challenges. The Metacritic page has no full list of bonuses, so we won’t guess at one. What matters is that reviewers call them a nice bonus, not a reason to buy. The reason to buy is the game itself on the new Nintendo console.

Worth Playing writes that the port “doesn’t disappoint”, and Gfinity says you “can’t go wrong” with the Switch 2 version. That sounds more modest than Nintendo Life, but it is also more honest: the technical result is judged reliable, with no loud caveats.

FRAGLINE OFFICIAL’s take

The best part of this story is that the score is built from matching opinions: scores of 90-plus from seven different outlets are not the outlier of one enthusiastic reviewer. For a port of an older remake to a new platform, that result says Capcom did not treat Switch 2 as a second-rate platform.

The doubtful part is the sample size. Sixteen reviews is not many, and a noticeable share of them come from outlets focused on Nintendo, which are all the more welcoming of a big game appearing on its console. Once the number of reviews grows substantially, the average could well slip by a couple of points, as scores usually do after launch. We are not claiming that will happen, but it is sensible to plan for it.

For comparison: on the same day Capcom released Resident Evil 4: Gold Edition, which got an 89 from 17 reviews. A four-point gap between two games from the same publisher shows that critics are rating the result of a specific port, not the logo. A second benchmark is the Switch 2 Ocarina of Time remake at 96: the console already has a couple of truly strong releases this autumn, and RE2 joins that group with confidence. The forecast is simple: if the user score holds around 8.6, the Switch 2 version will become one of the arguments for the console among those who were waiting for big multiplatform games.

Bottom line: critics agree this is one of the best ways to play Resident Evil 2 today, especially on a handheld. But 93 is a Metascore across sixteen texts, not a sentence. Let’s wait for the sample to grow.
